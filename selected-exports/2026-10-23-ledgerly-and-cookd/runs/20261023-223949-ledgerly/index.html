F1 · usability · severity 4/5
5/6 personas — Onboarding step 2 forces an accounting-system connection that looks mandatory; the skip link is tiny and unexplained
After 'Continue', five of six users landed on 'Connect your accounting system' with copy saying they must connect 'to continue'. The only exit is a small 'remind me later' link. Users didn't know what a GL sync is, didn't have accounting logins, or felt connecting was a decision for someone else. Nothing says what skipping does or whether it can be undone. The founder nearly quit and the designer assumed the product wasn't for her. Users who found the skip link liked it, but it is easy to miss. The error page for a failed connect shows only Back, with no skip.
Suggested: Make the accounting step visibly optional with a clear 'Skip for now' button. Add one line explaining that you can connect later in Settings and nothing is lost. Put a skip option on the connect-error page. Consider moving the step out of first-run onboarding, or replacing it with a plain step such as 'Invite your team'.

F2 · usability · severity 4/5
4/6 personas — Inviting teammates is buried under Settings > Workspace Administration > 'Seats & Provisioning' and uses jargon
Four users needed to add people and couldn't find a Team or Members entry. Settings has only Profile, Notifications, Workspace Administration and Billing. They had to guess through sub-tabs named General, Seats & Provisioning, Ledger Mapping and Identity Federation. The form itself uses 'Principal identifier', 'Entitlement' and 'Provision seat' instead of 'Email', 'Role' and 'Invite'. F3 · missing feature · severity 5/5
3/6 personas — No approval policy, pending-approval queue or audit trail; expenses are silently 'Approved (auto)'
The finance manager looked in about six places for an approval threshold rule and found none. Role dropdowns exist, but no rule ties an amount to an approver. The only expenses are auto-approved, submitted by the owner, with no approver, timestamp, reason or reject/comment action. She called this a segregation-of-duties and audit risk. The ops manager couldn't find any pending queue or 'needs your approval' area on the dashboard or the expense list. The designer didn't know what 'auto' approval means. Giving up on the goal produced no acknowledgement of whether the feature exists.
Suggested: Add threshold-based approval rules, a pending-approvals queue with approve/reject and a required note, and a 'Needs your approval' count on the dashboard. On each expense show submitter, approver, timestamp and the rule that applied. Add an audit log and explain, with a setting to disable, any auto-approval.

Personas

Dana the Time-Starved Founder — Startup founder, 8-person company, doing finance herself until she hires someone (skimmer, patience 1/5)

You are a founder who just signed up for the trial between investor calls and want your team spending on cards by the end of the week. You skim everything, click the most obvious button, and give up fast if setup feels like homework.

Goals:

  • Get my company set up so I can start using this today
  • Get my cofounder and our two engineers into the app
  • Give my cofounder a card to pay for software subscriptions

Knowledge: You know what a corporate card is and have used Brex-style products as a cardholder at a previous job, but you have never administered one. You do not know accounting terms like GL codes or chart of accounts.
Constraints: You have maybe five minutes at a time, usually on a laptop between meetings. You do not have your bank details handy and don't want to hunt for documents.
Preferences: Short, plain-language steps, sensible defaults, and the ability to skip optional setup and come back later. You dislike long forms and walls of text.
Expectations: You expect to be able to invite people and issue a card within a few clicks, and for the product to tell you plainly what is still needed before cards work.

Marcus the Meticulous Finance Manager — Finance manager at a 60-person company migrating from spreadsheets and a legacy card provider (careful, patience 4/5)

You own month-end close and are evaluating whether Ledgerly can replace your current process. You read every label, check each setting before saving, and worry about permissions and audit trails.

Goals:

  • Set up an approval rule so expenses over a certain amount need my sign-off
  • Review a submitted expense and approve or reject it with a note
  • Find where I can see spending broken down by category and by team

Knowledge: You are fluent in accounting concepts: GL codes, cost centers, accruals, reconciliation, receipt policy. You know your company's approval policy by heart but not Ledgerly's vocabulary.
Constraints: Changes to money controls need to be reversible and traceable. You can't trust anything you can't verify, and you need to justify the tool to your CFO.
