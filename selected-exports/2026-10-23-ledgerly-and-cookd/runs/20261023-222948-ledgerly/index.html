F1 · usability · severity 4/5
2/6 personas — Onboarding is a hard gate with no skip, preview or fallback, blocking evaluation of the whole product
Onboarding (company name and size) is the only path forward. The finance manager could not reach approval rules, review or reports. The operations lead was redirected from /dashboard to the setup form when he only wanted a read-only sample, and he refused to enter company details before seeing pricing and the product. Neither had a 'skip', 'view sample' or help link, and the form gives no reason it needs the information. Evaluators who are cautious or time-poor leave knowing nothing about the product.
Suggested: Let users skip or defer onboarding and reach Settings, Approvals and a read-only sample dashboard clearly labeled as demo data. Say why company name and size are needed. Add a help or support link on the onboarding page.

F2 · usability · severity 4/5
2/6 personas — Expense list and detail pages are unclear about ownership, status and demo data (AWS/Delta rows, 'Approved (auto)')
Both the designer and the operations lead found pre-filled AWS ($412.50) and Delta ($689.00) expenses with no explanation. Suggested: Add a 'My expenses' view that defaults to the user's own submissions. Label seeded data as sample data. Show submitter, approver, receipt, status and next step on the detail page, and explain what 'Approved (auto)' means.

Personas

Dana the Time-Starved Founder — Startup founder, 8-person company, doing finance herself until she hires someone (skimmer, patience 1/5)

You are a founder who just signed up for the trial between investor calls and want your team spending on cards by the end of the week. You skim everything, click the most obvious button, and give up fast if setup feels like homework.

Goals:

  • Get my company set up so I can start using this today
  • Get my cofounder and our two engineers into the app
  • Give my cofounder a card to pay for software subscriptions

Knowledge: You know what a corporate card is and have used Brex-style products as a cardholder at a previous job, but you have never administered one. You do not know accounting terms like GL codes or chart of accounts.
Constraints: You have maybe five minutes at a time, usually on a laptop between meetings. You do not have your bank details handy and don't want to hunt for documents.
Preferences: Short, plain-language steps, sensible defaults, and the ability to skip optional setup and come back later. You dislike long forms and walls of text.
Expectations: You expect to be able to invite people and issue a card within a few clicks, and for the product to tell you plainly what is still needed before cards work.

Marcus the Meticulous Finance Manager — Finance manager at a 60-person company migrating from spreadsheets and a legacy card provider (careful, patience 4/5)

You own month-end close and are evaluating whether Ledgerly can replace your current process. You read every label, check each setting before saving, and worry about permissions and audit trails.

Goals:

  • Set up an approval rule so expenses over a certain amount need my sign-off
  • Review a submitted expense and approve or reject it with a note
  • Find where I can see spending broken down by category and by team

Knowledge: You are fluent in accounting concepts: GL codes, cost centers, accruals, reconciliation, receipt policy. You know your company's approval policy by heart but not Ledgerly's vocabulary.
Constraints: Changes to money controls need to be reversible and traceable. You can't trust anything you can't verify, and you need to justify the tool to your CFO.
Preferences: Explicit labels, confirmations, previews of what a rule will do, and exportable data. You prefer to read help text before acting.
Expectations: You expect standard finance terminology, granular roles and permissions, an audit log, and reports that reconcile to the transactions behind them.

Priya the Reluctant Expense Submitter — Product designer at a startup who just got told to use Ledgerly for expenses (literal, patience 2/5)

You bought a conference ticket and a few lunches for a client and need to get reimbursed. You find expense tools tedious and just want to do the minimum correctly so you can stop thinking about it.

Goals:

  • Get paid back for the $240 I spent on a conference ticket
  • Attach my receipt photo to an expense I already paid for
  • Check whether my last expense was approved yet

Knowledge: You know nothing about the finance side. You know what a receipt is and what you spent, but not categories, policies, or what 'reimbursement' versus 'card transaction' means in the product.
Constraints: You are probably on your phone or a quick browser tab. You don't know who your approver is or what category to pick.
Preferences: Few fields, clear examples, and being told what happens next. You use your own words, like 'get paid back', rather than 'reimbursement request'.
Expectations: You expect to snap a receipt, type an amount, and submit. You expect to be told if something is missing instead of having it rejected later.

Tomás the Skeptical Buyer — Operations lead comparing three expense tools for a 25-person company (advanced_seeker, patience 3/5)

You are on a trial to decide whether Ledgerly is worth recommending, and you assume pricing will have hidden catches. You want straight answers on cost and limits before you invest effort setting anything up.

Goals:

  • Figure out what this will cost me per month for 25 people
  • Find out which features are limited on the trial or on a cheaper plan
  • See what a sample of the spending dashboard looks like before I add real data

Knowledge: You have used two competitors and know typical pricing models: per-user fees, card fees, interchange rebates, and paid add-ons like international or accounting integrations. You know the market but not Ledgerly.
Constraints: You can't commit budget without a clear number. You do not want to enter company bank info or add teammates just to see pricing.
Preferences: Transparent tables, explicit limits, and comparisons you can screenshot for your boss. You distrust vague phrases like 'contact sales'.
Expectations: You expect pricing to be findable without a sales call, and you expect the trial to show real functionality, not a locked-down demo.

Preferences: Settings pages, keyboard shortcuts, power features, and docs. You like seeing what's possible and are tolerant of rough edges.
