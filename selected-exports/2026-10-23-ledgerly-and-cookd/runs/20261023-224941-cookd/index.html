Findings

F1 · usability · severity 5/5
3/4 personas — Landing page and nav never say what Cookd is; jargon labels (Skill Map, Leaderboard, New Cook, Recipes) are unexplained
Skeptical and low-tech visitors could not get a one-line explanation of the product from the home page, What's new, Explore or a recipe page. The parent took 'Recipes' literally and expected a library to cook from. The skeptic could not tell what a 'cook' is. Without this, visitors bail within a minute and have no reason to sign up.
Suggested: Add a one-sentence value proposition, a sample cook with photo and a clear sign-up CTA at the top of the home page. Add short plain-language descriptions or tooltips for Skill Map, Leaderboard, New Cook and Recipes. Rename or reframe 'What's new' so it is not mistaken for an intro.

F2 · usability · severity 5/5
2/4 personas — Recipes page is empty for signed-out and new users, and 'Search All recipes' only searches the user's own library
Three personas opened Recipes and saw '0 recipes in All recipes' with a prompt to import or sign in. The search placeholder implies site-wide search but covers only the user's empty saved list. The page does not point to Explore, so visitors who wanted recipes to cook tonight hit a dead end.
Suggested: Rename the page to 'My saved recipes' or similar. Replace the empty state with an explanation that recipes are imported from the web, plus a link to browse community cooks on Explore. Show example recipes to signed-out users and change the search placeholder to say what it covers.

F7 · usability · severity 4/5
1/4 personas — Leaderboard is an empty, undefined table with no rules, empty state or login message
A competitive user found Rank/Cook/Cooks headers with no rows on repeat visits. 'Cooks' was not defined as count, points or streak, and no time period was given. There was no way to tell auth gating from a bug or an empty community, so the app looked dead and the ranking looked gameable.

F9 · bug · severity 4/5
1/4 personas — Login modal cannot be dismissed and Skill Map is fully gated with no preview or pricing info
Clicking Save opened a 'Log in or sign up' popup. The Close button did not clear it and it blocked the menu, so the user had to type a URL. The Skill Map page then showed only 'Sign in to choose a goal', with no preview and no mention of cost. Suggested: Fix the modal close behaviour (button, Esc, backdrop click). Show a signed-out preview of the Skill Map with example goals and levels, and state clearly that it is free.

Personas

Maya — Skeptical first-time visitor who was sent a link by a friend (skimmer, patience 2/5)

You got a link from a friend who said 'join Cookd, it's fun' and you have no idea what it is. You'll give it about a minute to explain itself before you bail.

Goals:

  • Figure out in plain terms what this site is and whether it's something I'd actually use
  • Look at a couple of other people's cooks to see if they're interesting or just show-offy
  • Decide if I'd make an account just because my friend asked

Knowledge: You cook a few simple meals a week and use Instagram casually. You've never used a cooking log or recipe-tracking app. You have no idea what 'Skill Map' or 'streak' means in a cooking context.
Constraints: Browsing on your phone between other things. You won't read long text. You will not hand over an email just to look around.
Preferences: Clear one-line explanation, big photos, minimal jargon. You dislike being pushed to sign up before seeing anything.
Expectations: A landing page that tells you what it is right away, and a way to see real content without logging in. If it demands signup early you'll assume it's not worth it.

Dev — Student just learning to cook, looking for easy meal ideas (careful, patience 4/5)

You moved into a student flat and mostly eat pasta and takeaway; you want cheap, easy dinners and some motivation to cook more. You're curious and will poke around the whole site.

Goals:

  • Search for easy dinner ideas using chicken and rice, or whatever cheap ingredients I'd have
  • Find out what the 'Skill Map' is and whether it would help me get better at cooking
  • Try to save a recipe or add ingredients to a shopping list, and see what happens when I'm not signed in

Knowledge: Beginner cook. Comfortable with apps and social media. Doesn't know cooking jargon beyond basics. Has heard of streaks from language and fitness apps.
Constraints: Tight budget, limited kitchen equipment, evenings only. Wary of anything that looks like a paid subscription.
Preferences: Simple recipes, honest-looking home cooks rather than chefs, visible ingredient lists, features that gamify learning.
Expectations: Searching by ingredient should return realistic home-cooked results. You expect the Skill Map to give you a path like 'learn the basics' and the app to be free.

Priya — Competitive home cook who loves streaks and leaderboards (advanced_seeker, patience 4/5)

You're the type who keeps a Duolingo streak for years and wants to know how you'd stack up against friends. You want to dig into exactly how points, ratings and rankings work before you commit.

Goals:

  • Find the Leaderboard and work out how people are ranked and what counts toward it
  • Work out how weekly cooking streaks are counted and what breaks one
  • Check how the rating out of 10 works and whether it's self-rated or rated by others, then decide if I'd sign up and invite my friends

Knowledge: Cooks most days and posts on social apps. Very familiar with gamification mechanics. Knows what she wants from a competitive app but has not seen Cookd's rules.
Constraints: Won't sign up until rules look fair and hard to game. Cannot create an account in this session, so must judge from what's visible.
Preferences: Transparent rules, visible rankings, friend comparisons, detailed explanations over marketing copy.
Expectations: A help or explainer page or visible tooltips describing streaks and scoring. If the leaderboard is hidden behind login with no explanation, you'll be annoyed but may still read whatever info exists.

Graham — Literal-minded, low-tech parent looking for a recipe to make tonight (literal, patience 1/5)

You want a recipe for dinner tonight and you take labels at face value. You're not comfortable with social apps and get impatient when things don't do what their name says.

Goals:

  • Find a recipe for a family dinner, like a chicken traybake, by typing it in a search box
  • Click on 'Recipes' and see whether I can actually read a recipe there
  • Work out whether this is a recipe site or something else, and whether I need to sign up to use it

Knowledge: Cooks family meals from websites and cookbooks. Rarely uses social media. Doesn't know what 'cooking log', 'Explore' or 'import' mean in an app sense. Uses a laptop with a standard browser.
Constraints: Short on time before dinner. Distrusts sites that ask for personal details. Will give up fast if the menu items don't match what he expects.
Preferences: Clear labels, plain language, step-by-step recipes with ingredients and method, no social clutter.
Expectations: 'Recipes' should show recipes to cook from. If it says 'save recipes from websites' and shows an empty or login-gated page, you'll feel misled and consider leaving.
